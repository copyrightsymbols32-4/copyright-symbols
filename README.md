## What is Copyright Symbols?

[Copyright Symbols](https://copyrightsymbols.com/) is a free online tool for copying copyright and trademark symbols. The copyright symbol © — a capital C inside a circle, Unicode U+00A9 — is a public notice that a piece of work has an owner. You will see it in website footers, books, apps, blog posts, photos, music, and videos.

The site also covers the symbols people often confuse with ©, so you can pick the one that matches your content.

| © | Copyright Symbol | U+00A9 | `&copy;` | Show copyright protection for creative work |
| ® | Registered Trademark | U+00AE | `&reg;` | A brand or logo that is formally registered |

## How to write a copyright notice

A clean copyright notice usually has three parts: the copyright sign, the year of first publication, and the copyright owner's name.

```
© 2026 Your Brand Name. All rights reserved.
```

Other common forms:

- Date range: `© 2020–2026 Your Company Name. All rights reserved.`
- Word form: `Copyright © 2026 Your Brand Name.`
- Simple footer: `© 2026 Brand Name`

Use your real brand, company, or creator name, add the first publication year, keep the notice readable, and place it where people can see it — usually the footer of every page, the end of a blog post, or the first or last page of a PDF. Use the same format across your site.

## © vs ® vs ™

- Use **©** for creative works: articles, books, photos, music, videos, and software.
- Use **™** for a brand claim on goods, and **℠** for a brand claim on services.
- Use **®** only for a trademark that has actually been registered.

The © mark supports copyright protection, but it is not magic armor on its own — it works best with clear ownership, dates, and, where needed, formal registration.
